Design that meets variation without sorting people. Park benches, toilet signs, lifts and counters that work for whoever arrives.

What it is

This collection shows design that meets human variation without sorting people first. The designs do not announce themselves. When design starts from variation, it needs no marker, no special route, and no symbol saying who it is for.

The park benches are my favourite example. A round table with gaps between the seats is not an accessible table. It is a table. The gap works for a wheelchair, a stroller, a walker, or a dog on a leash, and for the people around the table it is simply a place to sit.

The signs in this collection describe the room or the function instead of the visitor. A toilet seen from above, a wall grip named in words, a door panel showing what it does. It is the shift from person to function, photographed as I found it.
