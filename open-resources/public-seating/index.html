What it is

A seat in public space is a small statement about who is expected to stay. I photograph benches, tables, and chairs because they make that statement in shape rather than words. A table with one open place expects someone on wheels. A bench that circles the table without a gap does not.

The photographs run from seating that holds a place open, through seating that sorts its sitters, to seating built so that nobody can stay long. None of it carries a sign about who it is for.

I use these photographs in teaching alongside the red carpets. A carpet laid over stairs shows that a gesture of welcome can shut people out. These benches and tables show that furniture can do the same. The shape decides who gets a seat and who stays outside the circle, without a word or a symbol.
