Design beyond
“us” and “them”

We are all part of human variation. Every single I. Every single you. We all already belong. Nonclusive design starts from that and designs for it. What goes on in a design process influences both its outcome and its societal impact. Start from a category, and you risk designing only for that category. Start from variation, and you create room for exploring the connection between human difference and design flexibility. Beyond fixed categories. Beyond “us” and “them”. This site is about what that looks like in practice.

What nonclusion is

02 / 08

Nonclusion is a contribution to Universal Design,
not an alternative to it.

Universal Design targets how we can make things usable by as many people as possible. Nonclusion contributes a starting point for that work. We are all already part of human variation, so design can build for all of us from the start.

In practice, nonclusive design does three things. It designs for situations, where people and environments meet, rather than for groups of people. It lets signs and spaces communicate what they offer, rather than trying to pinpoint bodies, persons and roles. And it meets variation with variation: several kinds of seating, several ways to read, several ways to interact, several ways through a building.

Nonclusion is ultimately about societal development, and asks what kind of world we are building. It is a contribution to Universal Design. Design influences us every day, in all situations, often without words. It tells people whether they are expected and whether they count as part of a norm or as deviating from it. Design that starts from human variation moves development processes beyond “us” and “them”, one design decision, one entrance, one sign, one website at a time. Most of this site is about what that looks like in practice.
