The AUUD Matrix

A 3×3 matrix for evaluating Accessibility, Usability, and Universal Design: what is evaluated, and at which level.

What it is

The matrix is a thinking tool, not a compliance checklist. An early version appeared in newsletter edition 15, where I asked readers what they would put in the cells. The columns say what is being evaluated: Accessibility, Usability, and Universal Design. The rows say at which level: Output, Process, and Societal Impact.

  • Output is what gets built or made.
  • Process is how it was planned, designed and decided, and who took part.
  • Societal Impact is what it does to society over time.

Most evaluation of Universal Design today happens in the output row. Does the building meet the building regulations, does the website meet WCAG? That is mainly accessibility and usability evaluation. The two shaded cells are well charted. Many of the open questions are in the seven other cells, and the lower right corner, Universal Design at the societal level, is perhaps the most interesting starting point.

The matrix rests on the argument in the paper with Ståhl and Iwarsson that accessibility, usability, and Universal Design are distinct and complementary, and that all three need to be evaluated. For the societal-impact row we take inspiration from the Positive Peace framework, which measures peace through the conditions that make peace possible rather than the absence of violence alone. None of those conditions is peace, and the same logic may apply to Universal Design.

The matrix comes from a paper with Lilian Müller, Larry McNutt and Gerald Craddock, presented at UD26, the 8th International Universal Design Conference, in Galway in November 2026. At the same conference we run a workshop where the participants fill the cells with questions, thoughts, and candidate indicators. The reasoning behind the matrix is on the key ideas page.

Print the matrix, put it on a table, and fill it in. The cells are empty on purpose.

The matrix is also available as an online form. It has the same nine cells, and you can fill in as many as you like from a phone or a computer. I made it for the UD26 workshop as a third way to contribute to the AUUD evaluation, besides the printed poster and the tactile version. All contributions go into the same dataset.

The dataset

The pilot tests and the UD26 workshop produce material for the matrix: the questions, thoughts, and candidate indicators the participants put in the cells. That material will be published here as an open dataset after the conference, under the same CC BY 4.0 license. This page is its permanent address.

How to cite

APA

In Proceedings of UD26, the 8th International Universal Design Conference, Galway, 4–6 November 2026. IOS Press. Accepted manuscript; proceedings forthcoming.
